# Composing-Digital-Media-Reading-Critique
The reading that I will be reflecting on is The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction by Walter Benjamin. Going over this work, one of the concepts that he consistently returns to is the idea of aura. According to him, aura is an attribute that every work of art has which is tied to its uniqueness and authenticity. He describes it as  “the unique phenomenon of a distance, however close it may be” giving the interpretation that it is what separates the art from the observer, and how that unique identity is then perceived. In this text he goes on to explain how the modern age is experiencing a decay in aura going as far as saying,“That which withers in the age of mechanical reproduction is the aura of the work of art” and saying that from a generalized perspective, reproduction is responsible for this as it removes the art from the “domain of tradition” thus taking away its uniqueness and power of its experience.
Benjamin further explains the attributes of cult value and exhibition value, with cult value being the ritual aspect and exhibition value being the value of the action of experiencing it. More specifically, cult value is the observer using the art as a means of extracting something larger than just the piece of art, whether that be religious, philosophical, nostalgic, etc. Meanwhile, exhibition value is how much of an event is made out of seeing it. For example, one would argue a broadway musical has a bigger exhibition value when compared to watching a movie at home.
With these concepts in mind, I would like to apply them to the recent release of Avenger’s Endgame: Encore.  For those who may not know, in preparation for the film Avengers: Doomsday, which comes out later this year, Marvel Studios decided to rerelease their previous Avengers film (Avengers: Endgame) with more polished footage and extra post-credit scenes that the directors described as “crucial viewing” for its sequel. I think this makes for a very interesting discussion when looked at next to Benjamin’s writing. While I do think this movie is something that Benjamin would point to as proof of the loss of aura, due to the fact that it seems to be just a cash grab with a bunch of retcons sprinkled on top that seem to completely change the impact of the original version, there is something to be said for its cult and exhibition value. 
Following Endgame’s predecessor, Avengers: Infinity War, there was already a lot of anticipation for Endgame as its prequel ended on a jaw dropping cliff hanger after already pulling off one of the most ambitious crossovers in cinema history. As a result, the movie was able to pull in interest from a vast variety of fandoms and casual viewers alike, thus giving it a lot of exhibitional value where seeing the movie in theaters was seen as an event. What the re-release seems to be doing is the exact same thing as before except now with Doomsday while simultaneously playing on the feeling of nostalgia as they invite fans to revisit characters that they have been following for almost two decades. 
Overall, I think that Walter Benjamin would absolutely abhor the new release of Avenger’s Endgame: Encore. However, I also think that in many ways it helps validate many of his views and predictions of how consumerism and modernization affects art.  Marvel essentially uses each film as required context and setup for their next one while heavily relying and playing to the attributes of cult and exhibition value from Walter Benjamin’s writing.
